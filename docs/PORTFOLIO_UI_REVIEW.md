# Portfolio UI/UX comparison

Reviewed on 25 October 2026 against the current local portfolio. This is a visual and navigation review, not user testing or a ranking of engineering ability. The first three reference sites were opened in Chromium at desktop (1440px) and phone (390px) widths. No comparative performance or full accessibility audit was performed on the reference sites.

## References and comparison

| Reference | UI/UX observations | Comparison with Karam's portfolio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Brittany Chiang](https://brittanychiang.com/) | Restrained navy palette, persistent identity/navigation on desktop, three main section links and compact experience entries. Phone layout becomes a simple reading flow. | The closest reference for the recruitment purpose. Her fewer primary sections create stronger focus. Karam's opening screen makes CV downloading and contact more explicit, and the recent-experience index immediately identifies employers. The larger supporting catalogue makes Karam's page longer; the More disclosure and project filters help manage it. |
| [Josh W. Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/) | An illustrated, colourful desktop header establishes a strong visual identity. Categories, search and article links serve a publication-oriented homepage; mobile navigation is compact. | His site has a more recognisable visual signature. Karam's employment-first hierarchy better exposes job-application actions on the opening screen. The useful lesson is a coherent personal identity, rather than copying the blog structure or adding effects for their own sake. |
| [Lee Robinson](https://leerob.com/) | Serif typography, a mostly unadorned reading column, a large desktop illustration, and bio/notes/article navigation. The mobile view prioritises text. | His site demonstrates how restrained UI can still have character. Karam's dates, project screenshots, CV controls and recommendation letters provide more explicit recruitment evidence. Karam's page asks visitors to navigate more sections; Lee's homepage gives reading a clearer priority. |

## Additional concept reference

[Bruno Simon](https://bruno-simon.com/) describes his portfolio as a 3D world explored by driving, with keyboard, touch and gamepad controls. It connects the interaction directly to his creative web-development work. Its interface is a useful example of a portfolio acting as a demonstration of its author's specialism. Karam's conventional links offer a more direct route to employment information. The 3D scene did not fully initialise in the review browser, so no detailed visual, accessibility or performance comparison is claimed.

## Assessment for this portfolio

Karam's current site has a credible recruitment structure: visible contact and CV actions, experience before projects, real project screenshots, project filtering, explicit source/demo links and supporting evidence. Its larger number of sections makes it less focused than the simplest references. Its visual identity also remains more conventional than the illustrated or interactive examples; an accent colour alone does not create the same degree of distinction.

Keep the current layout and the shorter phone spacing. The approved warm accent provides continuity with the earlier palette while leaving the reading surfaces and typography intact. Avoid inferring that colourful sites are inherently more personal, or that famous developers' publishing and demonstration sites share the same goals as an early-career recruitment portfolio.

No extra career copy, new project imagery, project reordering or CV changes were made for this comparison. Future changes to imagery, content depth or page structure require a separate decision from the author.

## Colour implementation

- Light-mode warm accent: burnt orange `#a44723`; white foreground, approximately 5.99:1 contrast.
- Dark-mode warm accent: peach `#efa979`; deep ink `#101e24` foreground, approximately 8.61:1 contrast.
- Warm elements: primary contact actions, selected navigation and the small rule above recent experience.
- Retained: teal/mint ordinary links and focus, background/text colours, font, geometry, original media and all public data.
- Browser comparisons matched the saved introduction/experience geometry and body colours at 390px and 1440px in both themes.
